A Basement Apartment for a Parent in Utah, With a Bath That Works

What Utah's ADU law allows, and what the bathroom needs.

Call 1-888-779-2284Request a written quote

Direct answer: Since 2021, Utah law has made an internal accessory dwelling unit, such as a basement apartment inside an owner-occupied single-family home, a permitted use in most residential zones. For an aging parent, the bathroom is the hard part: a permitted plumbing job, a drain that may need a pump, a delivery path down the stairs, and a sleeping room with a code-sized escape window.

Utah families have long made room for a parent downstairs. The 2021 change in state law made that easier to do legally, but the law was written mainly with rental units in mind, and it says nothing about grab bars, thresholds or how a 70-year-old gets into a bathtub. This guide connects the two: what the state and your city can require, and how to plan a basement bathroom that an older adult can actually use. What does Utah's internal ADU law allow?

Utah Code 10-9a-530 covers internal accessory dwelling units in cities and towns. An internal ADU is a unit created inside a primary dwelling, within its existing footprint. The primary dwelling has to be a detached single-family home that the owner lives in as a primary residence. An attached garage counts if it is habitable and shares a wall with the house.

In areas zoned primarily for residential use, one internal ADU is a permitted use, so a city cannot simply refuse it. It can still set some conditions:

What a Utah city can and cannot require for an internal ADU
TopicWhat state law allows a city to do
Building, health and fire codesRequire full compliance.

Local practice still varies a lot. Herriman, for example, requires a signed, notarized owner-occupancy affidavit, one 9-by-18-foot paved parking stall unless the home already has four, a business license for any rented ADU, and a building permit for structural changes, with a $559 review fee plus permit fees. Call your own city's planning office before you design anything.

One honest wrinkle: the statute defines an internal ADU as a unit offered as a long-term rental. A parent who lives downstairs without paying rent may fall outside that definition, but the building code still applies to any new bathroom, kitchen or bedroom. Ask the city how it treats a family suite.

Is a basement the right place for an aging parent?

Sometimes, and it is worth being blunt about when it is not. The stairs are the whole question. A parent who manages stairs well today may not in three years, and a basement apartment that requires a flight of stairs to reach the kitchen, the laundry or the car becomes a trap after a hip fracture or a stroke.

  • Good fit: a walkout basement with a level or ramped exterior door, a parent who is steady on stairs, and a plan for later.
  • Possible fit: interior stairs with sturdy rails on both sides and room for a stair lift if needed.
  • Poor fit: a parent who already uses a walker or wheelchair, or who has fallen on stairs. A main-floor bedroom and bathroom is usually the better plan, and the basement can go to someone else in the household.

What does a basement bedroom need under the building code?

Any basement sleeping room needs an emergency escape and rescue opening, usually an egress window. The minimums come from section R310 of the International Residential Code, which Utah adopts with state amendments. They matter for an older adult in a practical way: the window is the second way out in a fire, and a sill near the 44-inch maximum is a hard climb for someone with limited mobility. If you are adding a well anyway, build in permanent steps and keep the sill lower than the maximum where you can.

What makes a basement bathroom work for an older adult?

A basement is a different job from a main-floor bathroom. These are the questions that change the plan and the price.

Basement bathroom checklist for an aging parent
QuestionWhy it matters
Is the floor below the sewer line?If so, waste water has to be pumped up. A walk-in tub empties a large volume at once, so the pump and basin must be sized for it.
Is there a rough-in already?Many Utah basements have capped drain stubs. If not, the slab has to be cut and trenched for drains, which adds time and cost.
Will the tub or shower fit down the stairs?A one-piece walk-in tub does not flex. Measure stair width, headroom and every turn on the way down.
Where is the water heater?Often in the basement, which is good: short hot water runs fill a tub faster and warmer.
Is there enough ceiling height?Basement ceilings and ductwork can limit a shower surround or a raised platform.
How will moisture leave the room?Below-grade bathrooms need a properly vented exhaust fan to keep humidity off cool foundation walls.
Is the path from bed to bathroom level and lit?Night trips are when falls happen. Short, straight and lit is the goal.

A curbless roll-in shower is often easier than a tub in a basement because the slab can be recessed for a zero-threshold entry. A walk-in tub suits a parent who wants to sit and soak, or who cannot stand for a shower. Either way, a factory-certified tech does a full site visit before install to confirm the drain, the path and the electrical.

Basement apartment and accessible bathroom questions

Is a basement apartment allowed in Utah?

In most cities, yes. Utah Code 10-9a-530 makes an internal accessory dwelling unit, one built inside the footprint of an owner-occupied single-family home, a permitted use in areas zoned primarily residential. Cities can still require building code compliance, an extra parking space, a rental permit, and can bar them on lots of 6,000 square feet or less.

Do I need a permit to add a bathroom in my basement in Utah?

Plan on it. A new bathroom adds plumbing and usually electrical work, which goes through your city's building permit process whether or not the space is rented. Herriman, for example, requires a building permit for any structural change or remodel tied to an internal ADU.

How big does a basement bedroom egress window have to be?

Under the International Residential Code, an emergency escape opening needs at least 5.7 square feet of net clear opening (5 square feet at or below grade), at least 24 inches high and 20 inches wide, with the sill no more than 44 inches above the floor. A below-grade window needs a well of at least 9 square feet, and a ladder or steps if it is deeper than 44 inches.

Can a walk-in tub go in a basement bathroom?

Often, yes, if the drain works out. If the basement floor sits below the sewer line, the bathroom needs a pump to lift waste water, and a walk-in tub sends a lot of water at once. The tub also has to fit down the stairs and around any turn, so measure the path before choosing a model.

Is a basement apartment a good idea for an aging parent?

Only if the stairs are not a barrier, now and in a few years. A walkout basement with a level exterior door can work well. If stairs are already hard, a main-floor bedroom and bathroom is usually the safer plan, and the basement can house the rest of the family instead.
