Installation guide

Walk-in tub installation in Utah

Direct answer: When a walk-in tub replaces an existing tub in the same alcove, many installs finish in one day. Utah law requires a licensed plumbing contractor for work on a home's plumbing system, even on small jobs, so check any installer's license with the Division of Professional Licensing. Before you order, confirm your water heater can fill the tub in winter.

What install day looks like

  1. Before the day: the bathroom is measured or photographed, the model is confirmed in writing, and anything unusual (an old drain, a narrow hallway, a second-floor bath) is flagged.
  2. Protect and remove: floors and the path to the bathroom are covered, then the old tub is taken out.
  3. Set the new tub: it is leveled, the drain and water supply are connected, and wiring for jets or a heater is hooked up if the model has them.
  4. Fill test: the tub is filled with the door closed and checked for leaks, and every feature is run.
  5. Finish and walkthrough: wall panels or trim are fitted, the room is cleaned, and you are shown how to use the door, controls and drain.

Aging Safely Baths uses factory-certified installers in Utah and says most installs take one day, with tile work or older-home issues sometimes adding a second. You should be able to use the bathroom again once the installer leaves, unless your quote says otherwise.

Utah licensing rules

Utah lets unlicensed handymen take some small jobs, but plumbing is an exception. Under Utah Code 58-55-305, as amended by H.B. 483 in 2024, the handyman exemption covers projects under $7,000 in labor and materials. Even on those projects, work on a plumbing or electrical system must be done by a licensed plumbing or electrical contractor. Replacing a gas water heater must be done by a person certified for gas appliance work.

Replacing a bathtub means disconnecting and reconnecting the drain and the water supply, so plan on a licensed plumbing contractor. You can check any Utah license in about a minute with the state's DOPL license lookup. Search the business name, then confirm the license is active and covers plumbing.

Permits and power

Permit rules are set by your city or county building department. Many treat a like-for-like tub swap differently from a remodel that moves plumbing or adds a circuit. Ask your installer who pulls any permit and include it in the quote.

Tubs with jets or an inline heater usually need a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit. In an older Salt Lake City or Ogden home, the panel may be full or far from the bathroom, which adds electrician time. A soaker tub with no motors avoids this.

The winter hot-water check

You sit in a walk-in tub while it fills, so the water has to be warm enough the first time. Two things work against Utah homes in winter. Cold water entering the house is colder than in summer, so each gallon of bath water needs more hot water mixed in. And a storage water heater refills with that colder water, so it recovers more slowly.

Hot water needed for a 50-gallon bath at 100°F from a 120°F heater
Cold water coming inHot water neededShare of the bath
60°F (late summer)33 gallons67%
50°F36 gallons71%
40°F (cold winter supply)38 gallons75%

Simple mixing math for illustration: hot share = (bath temperature minus cold temperature) divided by (heater temperature minus cold temperature). Your real numbers depend on your tub, heater setting and pipes.

What to do with this:

  • Find the tub's capacity at bathing depth on its spec sheet.
  • Find the first-hour rating on your water heater's yellow EnergyGuide label or its data plate. That is roughly how much hot water it can deliver in a busy hour.
  • Measure your cold tap with a kitchen thermometer in January if you can.
  • If the numbers are close, ask about a larger heater or an inline tub heater, and avoid running the dishwasher or laundry before a bath.

If a gas water heater is replaced, the installer should also follow the manufacturer's instructions for high-altitude setup, since most of Utah sits well above sea level.

Supply-only delivery

If you have a plumber or remodeler you already trust, the tub can be shipped supply-only to any Utah address. You receive the spec sheets and rough-in dimensions in advance, and questions during the install can go to the Aging Safely Baths team by phone. It suits whole-bathroom remodels and families who live far from the Wasatch Front. Your contractor still needs the right Utah license for the plumbing work.

Questions to ask an installer

  • What is your Utah DOPL license number, and does it cover plumbing?
  • Will the tub fit through my doors and hallway, and who checks that?
  • Is my water heater big enough for this model in winter?
  • Does this model need a new circuit, and is that in the price?
  • Who pulls any permit, and is it included?
  • What happens if you find water damage behind the wall?
  • How many days will I be without this bathroom?
