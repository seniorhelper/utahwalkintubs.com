Older homes: what to expect

From the Avenues and Sugar House to Rose Park and Glendale, many Salt Lake City bathrooms date from before 1960, and 67.9% of the city's homes predate 1980. Bathrooms from those decades were often built around a heavy cast-iron tub, with plaster walls and older drain lines. When a walk-in tub goes in, the old tub usually has to be broken up to come out, the drain may need new parts, and the wall around the tub needs finishing.

That is ordinary work, not a reason to worry, but it belongs in your quote before the job starts. Ask specifically about the drain, wall repair and whether a second day might be needed. Narrow hallways and tight stairs in older homes also matter, because the new tub has to fit through them. Our Utah cost guide lists every line item to ask for.

What does a walk-in tub cost in Salt Lake City?

Aging Safely Baths publishes the same installed prices statewide: $15,250 to $20,000 for a walk-in tub and $13,500 to $17,000 for a roll-in shower system, after current promotions. Where a Salt Lake City job lands in that range depends mostly on the house, not the ZIP code.

Do you need a permit for a walk-in tub in Salt Lake City?

Usually, if plumbing or wiring changes. Salt Lake City Building Services says installing or altering plumbing or electrical systems requires a permit, while painting, flooring and other cosmetic work does not. Applications go through the city's Citizen Access Portal, and Building Services answers status questions at 801-535-6000.

Two city rules are worth knowing. The owner of an owner-occupied primary home may pull permits personally, with an Owner/Builder Certification. And the city points residents to the Utah Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL, 801-530-6628) to check a contractor's license, along with proof of insurance and bonding. Utah also requires a licensed plumbing contractor for the plumbing part of an install; our Utah installation guide covers that rule. Your installer pulls the permit where one is required.

If you rent

Only 46.8% of Salt Lake City homes are owner-occupied, so many older residents rent. A walk-in tub is a permanent fixture, so you need the owner's written permission first, and the owner should be part of the quote. If permission is not realistic, a transfer bench, grab bars installed into studs and a handheld shower can make the existing tub much safer at a fraction of the cost.

Salt Lake City water

Salt Lake City Public Utilities blends water from several mountain-stream treatment plants, wells and springs, and two water districts. In 2025 total hardness ranged from 136 mg/L at the Big Cottonwood plant to 212 mg/L at City Creek, and some wells reached 571 mg/L. In summer the city adds more well water to the mix. If your tub has water jets, plan for regular cleaning; our Utah hard water guide has the full table.

How a Salt Lake City install works

  1. Call or send photos. The tub, the faucet wall and the path from the front door, including stairs.
  2. Written, itemized quote. Model, removal, drain, wall finish, electrical and permits on separate lines. No high-pressure sales visit.
  3. Deposit and technician site visit. A factory-certified tech measures, photographs and checks the water heater and wiring.
  4. Install day. Most installs take one day; an older bathroom with wall or drain repair can take longer, and the quote should say so.
  5. Walkthrough. Door, controls and drain, for the bather and anyone who helps.

No bathing product eliminates the risk of falls; products are designed to reduce risk when installed and used properly.

Salt Lake City walk-in tub questions

How much is a walk-in tub in Salt Lake City?

Aging Safely Baths publishes $15,250 to $20,000 installed for walk-in tubs and $13,500 to $17,000 for roll-in showers, after current promotions. Older homes with cast-iron tubs, plaster walls or old drains tend to land higher in the range, so ask for an itemized written quote.

Do I need a permit to put in a walk-in tub in Salt Lake City?

If the job installs or alters plumbing or electrical systems, Salt Lake City requires a permit. Most walk-in tub installs touch the drain or valve, and jetted or heated tubs often need a new circuit. Your installer pulls the permit where one is required.

Can a walk-in tub go into a pre-1960 Salt Lake City bathroom?

Usually, yes. Expect the old cast-iron tub to be broken up for removal and ask about drain parts and wall finishing. Measure every doorway and stair turn on the way in, because a one-piece tub does not flex.

Will someone come to my home before the install?

Yes. The quote starts by phone or from photos with no high-pressure sales visit. After you choose a model and place a deposit, a factory-certified tech does a full site visit to measure and confirm everything.

Who helps Salt Lake City seniors pay for bathroom changes?

Start with Salt Lake County Aging and Adult Services at 385-468-3200 about the Medicaid Aging Waiver and county programs. Veterans can ask the VA Salt Lake City team about a HISA grant, up to $6,800 or $2,000 lifetime. Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in tubs.
